PBJT
in Dindigul,
Tamilnadu Southern India

Dindigul district, in the southern part of Tamil Nadu, sits at a unique ecological crossroads

Where fertile plains meet the hill ecosystems of the Palani and Sirumalai ranges. These landscapes play a defining role in shaping the district's climate, water systems, and biodiversity. The plains experience a semi-arid tropical monsoon climate, while hill areas such as Kodaikanal enjoy a cooler, more temperate environment. With an average annual rainfall of around 806 mm, largely dependent on the North East Monsoon, both agriculture and water availability remain highly climate-sensitive.

The district's varied soils—ranging from red loamy to black—support the cultivation of crops like onions, groundnuts, grapes, flowers, and vegetables. Nearly 70% of the population relies on agriculture and related activities for their livelihoods. Beyond farming, Dindigul has a strong base of traditional industries, including iron lock making, leather tanning, and textile production—especially the Arni Silk and Sungudi sarees of Chinnalapatti.
